Project Planning Guide

A structured guide to turning a project objective into a workable plan that teams can communicate, monitor, and update responsibly.

Start with the outcome and requirements

Clarify the intended outcome, who needs it, what problem it addresses, and how acceptance will be judged. Gather requirements and distinguish essential needs from preferences. Record assumptions, constraints, exclusions, dependencies, and unresolved questions.

Define scope and build a WBS

Project scope describes the work required to deliver the agreed result and its boundaries. A Work Breakdown Structure (WBS) decomposes the scope into smaller, manageable components. A useful WBS is deliverable-oriented. For each work package, clarify what is included, who is responsible, and how completion will be accepted.

Build a realistic schedule

List activities, estimate durations with the people doing the work, and identify dependencies. Common relationships include finish-to-start, start-to-start, finish-to-finish, and start-to-finish. Add milestones for decisions, approvals, and handovers.

The Critical Path Method (CPM) identifies the longest-duration path through a network of dependent activities, determining the shortest feasible project duration under the estimates and relationships used. A Gantt chart helps communicate activities, dates, dependencies, and progress.

Estimate and manage the budget

Estimate resources and costs for the planned work. Make assumptions explicit, distinguish one-time costs from ongoing costs, and consider contingency for identified uncertainty. Changes to an approved cost baseline should follow the project's change-control rules.

Plan quality, resources, and communication

Define measurable acceptance criteria and quality checks. Identify required skills, availability, equipment, and supplier contributions. Agree who needs which information, how often, in what format, and who is responsible. A RACI matrix can clarify who is responsible, accountable, consulted, and informed.

Monitor the plan

Compare progress and actual costs with agreed baselines. Record decisions, risks, issues, and approved changes. The Earned Value Management guide explains how planned value, earned value, and actual cost can be compared. Continue with the free project management study resource.
