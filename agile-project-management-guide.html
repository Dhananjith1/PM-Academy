Agile Project Management

An introductory explanation of adaptive delivery and how Scrum, Kanban, and hybrid approaches help teams manage changing work.

What does Agile project management mean?

Agile project management describes ways of organising and delivering work through short feedback loops, collaboration, learning, and adaptation. An adaptive team works towards an outcome while refining priorities as it learns. Agile does not mean “no planning” or “no documentation”.

Predictive, adaptive, and hybrid approaches

A predictive approach plans scope, sequencing, estimates, and controls in greater detail up front. An adaptive approach delivers in increments and revisits priorities as evidence changes. A hybrid approach combines practices from both. The right approach depends on uncertainty, regulation, dependencies, stakeholder needs, and the cost of change.

Scrum at a glance

Scrum is a lightweight framework for generating value through adaptive solutions to complex problems. A Scrum Team includes a Product Owner, Scrum Master, and Developers. It works in Sprints, which provide regular opportunities to inspect progress and adapt the plan.

  • Product Backlog: an ordered, evolving list of work needed to improve the product.
  • Sprint Planning: the team establishes why the Sprint is valuable, what can be done, and how the work will be approached.
  • Daily Scrum: Developers inspect progress towards the Sprint Goal and adapt their plan.
  • Sprint Review: the team and stakeholders inspect the outcome and discuss what to do next.
  • Sprint Retrospective: the team identifies ways to improve effectiveness and quality.

Use the current Scrum Guide for the framework's authoritative description.

Kanban and flow management

Kanban helps teams visualise work, make policies explicit, limit work in progress (WIP), and improve flow. WIP limits reduce excessive parallel work and make bottlenecks easier to see. Useful flow measures include work item age, cycle time, throughput, and work in progress.

How to choose an approach

Consider how frequently requirements change, how quickly users can provide feedback, how tightly work is coupled, what approvals are required, and how much uncertainty exists. Choose an approach that supports responsible delivery rather than adopting a method as a label.
