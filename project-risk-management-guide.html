Project Risk Management

A clear, repeatable approach to managing uncertainty before it becomes a surprise that disrupts project delivery.

What is a project risk?

A project risk is an uncertain event or condition that, if it occurs, can affect one or more project objectives. Risks can be threats or opportunities. An issue is different: it is a condition that has already happened and needs attention now.

Identify risks early and continuously

Review assumptions, requirements, estimates, suppliers, technology, dependencies, staffing, approvals, external changes, and handovers. Ask what could cause a delay, increase cost, reduce quality, affect adoption, or create an opportunity to deliver greater value. Involve people who understand the work.

Use a practical risk register

  • Risk statement: describe a possible cause, uncertain event, and potential effect.
  • Owner: identify who monitors and coordinates the response.
  • Assessment: record likelihood and impact using the agreed rating scale.
  • Response: list preventive actions, contingency actions, triggers, and due dates.
  • Status: show whether the risk is open, changing, closed, or has occurred as an issue.

Probability-impact matrices help prioritise discussion. Their scores are estimates for decision support, not precise predictions.

Choose a response that fits

For threats, strategies include avoiding the threat, reducing its likelihood or impact, transferring an agreed portion of its consequences, or accepting it while monitoring exposure. For opportunities, strategies may include exploiting, enhancing, sharing, or accepting the opportunity.

Example: a supplier may deliver late

If a supplier misses a confirmed delivery window, integration testing may start late and delay a release milestone. The team might assign an owner, confirm supplier milestones, identify an alternative source, and set a trigger for escalation.

Review, communicate, and adapt

Review significant risks regularly and after important changes. Retire risks that are no longer relevant, add newly discovered ones, and record when a risk becomes an issue. Risk management should support informed decisions, not create paperwork nobody uses.
