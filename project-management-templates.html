Free project management templates

Free Project Management Templates

A practical collection of simple project management templates and examples you can adapt for a school, community, business, or IT project. Replace the prompts with verified project information before using them.

1. Project charter template

A project charter summarises why a project exists, what it is expected to deliver, and who is authorised to guide the work. Keep it concise and confirm it with the appropriate sponsor or decision-maker.

  • Project name: [Enter the project name]
  • Purpose and business need: [What problem or opportunity does the project address?]
  • Objectives and success measures: [Describe measurable outcomes and how they will be checked]
  • Scope: [What is included?]
  • Out of scope: [What is explicitly excluded?]
  • Key deliverables: [List the main outputs]
  • Stakeholders and roles: [Sponsor, project manager, users, delivery team]
  • Milestones: [Milestone, target date, approval owner]
  • Assumptions, constraints, and initial risks: [Record what is known and uncertain]
  • Approval: [Approver, decision, date]

2. Project management plan template

Use this outline as a starting point for a project plan. The level of detail should match the project's size, uncertainty, and governance requirements.

  1. Overview: purpose, objectives, scope, exclusions, assumptions, and constraints.
  2. Scope management: requirements, deliverables, acceptance criteria, and Work Breakdown Structure (WBS).
  3. Schedule management: activities, estimates, dependencies, milestones, and schedule baseline.
  4. Cost management: estimates, approved budget, contingency assumptions, and cost tracking.
  5. Quality management: standards, reviews, testing, and acceptance responsibilities.
  6. Resource and communication plans: responsibilities, availability, stakeholder updates, and meeting cadence.
  7. Risk, issue, and change control: registers, escalation routes, decision authority, and change records.
  8. Delivery and closure: handover, training, support, acceptance, and lessons learned.

3. Work Breakdown Structure (WBS) example

A WBS breaks the approved project scope into deliverables and manageable work packages. This simplified example is for a small website project; change it to match the actual deliverables.

  • 1.0 Website project
    • 1.1 Requirements and approval
    • 1.2 User experience and visual design
    • 1.3 Website content and assets
    • 1.4 Build and configuration
    • 1.5 Testing and acceptance
    • 1.6 Release and handover

Do not confuse a WBS with a task list. The WBS organises scope; activities and dependencies are then developed to create the schedule.

4. Project risk register template

Use one row per risk. Write each risk as a possible cause, uncertain event, and potential effect. Ratings should use a scale agreed by the project team.

  • Risk ID and description: [Cause → uncertain event → possible effect]
  • Likelihood and impact: [Rating and brief rationale]
  • Risk owner: [Person responsible for monitoring the risk]
  • Response: [Avoid, reduce, transfer, or accept a threat; define actions]
  • Trigger and due date: [What would indicate the risk is changing?]
  • Status: [Open, monitoring, occurred as issue, or closed]

5. RACI matrix template

A RACI matrix clarifies who is Responsible for doing the work, Accountable for the result, Consulted for input, and Informed about progress. Agree the meanings with the team and normally assign one clearly accountable role for each activity.

  • Rows: key deliverables or decisions.
  • Columns: roles such as sponsor, project manager, technical lead, reviewer, and user representative.
  • Cells: R, A, C, or I as appropriate.

6. Project status report template

  • Reporting period: [Start and end date]
  • Overall status: [Explain the reason for the status]
  • Completed this period: [Accepted deliverables and verified progress]
  • Next steps: [Owner and target date]
  • Schedule and budget: [Baseline compared with current forecast]
  • Top risks and issues: [Impact, owner, response, and escalation needed]
  • Decisions required: [Decision, decision-maker, and due date]

7. RAID log: risks, assumptions, issues, and dependencies

A RAID log gives a team one place to review uncertainties, planning assumptions, active issues, and dependencies. Include an ID, description, owner, due date, response or action, and status for each item. Keep the categories distinct: an issue has already happened, while a risk is uncertain.

How to use free project management templates well

Templates are prompts, not substitutes for judgement. Remove sections that do not help, add project-specific controls where needed, protect confidential information, and obtain required approvals. Never present sample entries or estimated values as real project facts.
